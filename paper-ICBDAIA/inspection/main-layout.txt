       Hiding Images in Text and Text in Images with
                      Generative AI

                                     Anonymous Authors



       Abstract. A small grayscale image can be carried inside generated text, and a
       short message can be carried inside an image. The practical challenge is to recover
       the represented content from the file actually delivered, rather than from infor-
       mation retained by the sender. We implement a common authenticated recovery
       protocol for language and pixel autoregressive models and compare three coding
       rules under fixed carrier budgets. Receivers reconstruct standardized grayscale
       pixels or literal text bytes from saved UTF-8 and PNG files using agreed settings
       and a key. Across 120 prospective outcomes, fixed and entropy-gated coding re-
       cover every allocated payload in both directions. The finite-message arithmetic
       implementation recovers all PNG payloads but none of the text payloads before
       the text-length limit. Successful recovery does not imply concealment: the tested
       scores completely separate fixed and gated text from ordinary samples, while
       generated-PNG detection varies with coding rule and observer context. A sepa-
       rate development benchmark achieves a 5.13-fold latency reduction with checked
       output equivalence. An extension that changes selected photograph pixels also re-
       covers its messages, but a simple parity baseline has slightly better mean fidelity
       and substantially lower cost. These results distinguish encryption of content from
       hiding its presence and show that capacity, reproducible decoding, detectability
       and efficient execution require separate evaluation.

       Keywords: Generative steganography · Large language models · Image steganog-
       raphy · Exact recovery · Steganalysis.


1   Introduction

As a hypothetical illustration, a sender encodes a miniature grayscale image into a
generated paragraph. The recipient saves the paragraph and reconstructs its pixels using
agreed model settings and a key. In the reverse direction, a delivered PNG carries a
short text message. The hidden content is the payload; the delivered text or image is the
carrier. Encryption protects contents, whereas steganography concerns carrying them
within another medium without revealing their presence.
     Our question is: how reliably and efficiently can information be recovered across
generated text and image carriers, and what does that recovery tell us about detectabil-
ity? Generative models offer alternative text or pixel choices, but the receiver must
reproduce their information mapping from the delivered file. Sender-only token iden-
tifiers, cached ranks and intermediate packets cannot establish recovery through that
interface.
     We integrate existing coding ideas into a common saved-file protocol and compare
fixed-rank, entropy-gated and finite-message arithmetic coding on paired payloads. The
2


contribution is the shared protocol and controlled evaluation of recovery, detection
and cost. Separate observer-context, GPU and photograph extensions do not enlarge
the prospective sample. Exact image recovery means equality of standardized 16 × 16
grayscale pixels, not restoration of an arbitrary original image file. Successful decoding
establishes neither naturalness nor concealment.


2     Related Work
Calgacus transfers context-dependent token ranks and discusses cross-domain use [8].
A rank is a symbol’s position in the model’s ordered choices. RankCloak develops
bounded byte coding, entropy gating and replay practices, and reports rendered-text
recovery difficulties [6]. We inherit these foundations rather than claim rank transfer or
cross-domain transport as new.
     Neural Linguistic Steganography uses arithmetic decoding to generate text and en-
coding to recover bits [16]; Pixel-Stega applies arithmetic stegosampling to autoregres-
sive images [14]. Our independently implemented comparator has explicit finite-message
framing. Published stepwise verification addresses tokenization inconsistency [13]; we
adapt complete-prefix verification. GLTR provides precedent for probability and rank
scores [5], whereas our controls are ordinary samples from the same eligible model
distribution.
     Image embedding precedents include quantization index modulation, syndrome-
trellis codes, HiDDeN and StegaStamp [2,4,15,11]. Our photograph extension recon-
structs model partitions from coarse pixels and compares with simple parity. CUDA
graphs are established infrastructure [9]; we test acceleration while preserving decoding
behavior.


3     Recovery from Delivered Files
3.1   Shared Protocol and Receiver Boundary
The sender seals source bytes into an encrypted and authenticated packet and maps its
information to model choices. The receiver obtains only the saved carrier, profile, shared
context and key. The profile fixes models, tokenizer, numerical rules, coder and budget.
Source bytes/digests, sender token IDs, ranks and packets are excluded. After the sender
exits, a fresh receiver reconstructs and authenticates the packet; a separate evaluator
checks source equality (Fig. 1).
    Image payloads are 256 unsigned bytes: row-major 16 × 16 grayscale pixels. Text
payloads are literal UTF-8 without normalization, with a declared range of 32–128
bytes. An eight-byte header identifies version, type, source length and dimensions; a
256-byte source slot is zero padded. AES-256-GCM [3] encrypts and authenticates
these 264 bytes with protocol-bound associated data. A 12-byte nonce and 16-byte
tag make a fixed 292-byte, or 2,336-bit, carried packet. Keys and nonces use operating-
system randomness; paired coders reuse sealed ciphertext without re-encryption. Parsing
validates type, dimensions, length, UTF-8 and padding after authentication. Failure
returns no plaintext. This authenticates content, not concealment.

3.3   Coding, Capacity and Stopping

Fixed rank. Each packet byte is split into high and low four-bit groups. Value 𝑑 ∈
{0, . . . , 15} chooses rank 𝑑 + 1, so 584 positions carry the packet. The receiver inverts
those ranks. Fewer than 16 eligible symbols while bits remain causes a support failure.

Entropy-gated rank. Entropy gating uses fixed-rank coding only when the model offers
enough uncertainty, measured by

Otherwise an ordinary sample consumes a carrier position without payload bits. Both
endpoints recompute the gate. Development fixed 𝜏 ≈ 0.598397 bits for text and
3.215951 bits for channels; full-precision thresholds remain unchanged.

Arithmetic A1. Following [16], A1 begins with [0, 232 ) and partitions the current interval
according to 𝑞 𝑡 . At width 𝑅, it retains symbols with 𝑞 𝑡 (𝑣) ≥ 1/𝑅 and at least the highest-
probability min(2, |𝐸 𝑡 |, 𝑅) symbols. Renormalized widths are rounded to nearest integer,
ties to even; bins stop before cumulative overflow, residual width goes to the first symbol,
and zero-width bins are removed. The next 32 packet bits select a bin, with zero lookahead
only beyond the packet. Both endpoints emit common leading bits of the lower endpoint
and upper endpoint minus one, then shift the interval. A step can consume a symbol
without emitting bits.
     Completion is the first emission reaching 2,336 bits; up to 31 excess bits must
be zero and are discarded. There is no implicit flush or endpoint dump. This differs
from the reference’s final-token handling. A1 can stagnate when an unchanged narrow
interval straddles a leading-bit boundary; observed failures therefore require trace-based
diagnosis.
     Text has a 2,048-token cap and requires 32 ordinary tokens after packet completion.
A packet finishing too late for this tail is not a conforming carrier. PNG generation
fills all 2,976 channels after packet completion; neither metadata nor appended bytes
carry payload. Inversion follows inductively when observed prefixes reproduce identical
distributions and transitions. Serialization, sufficient support and capacity are separate
necessary conditions.


3.4   Extension to Existing Photographs

The photograph profile carries the same packet through a 256 × 256 RGB image. For
each channel, preserve 𝐵(𝑐) = 4b𝑐/4c. Both endpoints can then reconstruct the coarse
context 𝐵 + 2 from delivered pixels, without the original cover. PixelCNN++ evaluates
64 fixed 32 × 32 tiles. At each selected pixel, the 64 colors in its 4 × 4 × 4 cell are
ranked by joint conditional log probability, with lexicographic ties. Zero-based rank

parity forms two equal bit classes. The sender selects the nearest color by squared RGB
distance in the required class; the receiver reads its rank parity.
    Domain-separated HKDF/HMAC-SHA256 placement, derived from the key, coarse-
image hash and agreed protocol, selects 2,336 distinct pixel positions. It does not depend
on the hidden packet nonce. The baseline uses (𝑟 + 𝑔 + 𝑏) mod 2 with identical packet,
positions and nearest-distance rule. Channel changes are bounded by three. These coarse-
context ranks differ from generated-PNG likelihoods. The receiver needs PNG, profile
and key; unused pixels are not authenticated.

4     Experimental Design
4.1   Sources, Pairing and Uncertainty
The prospective allocation contains 20 held-out image payloads and 20 held-out text
payloads, each evaluated with all three coders: 20 × 2 × 3 = 120 outcomes. Images are
Fashion-MNIST test samples, two per class, BOX-resized from 28 × 28 to 16 × 16 [12].
Texts are nonoverlapping spans from Austen’s Pride and Prejudice and Doyle’s The
Adventures of Sherlock Holmes, from frozen Gutenberg sources: ten each in the 32–64
and 65–128-byte bands, with realized lengths 58–128 bytes and mean 93.2. Payload
sources were disjoint from development; sources, contexts and thresholds were frozen
before generation. Development contexts were reused. The text prompt requests a calm
forest field journal; the PNG row comes from an independent development image.
    One sealed packet is shared by the three methods within each payload/context group.
Failures are retained. Each group owns an ordinary control: a 2,048-token text trace with
length-matched prefixes, or a full PNG. These are 40 independent controls; reuse does
not create observations.
    Goodput counts useful source bits actually delivered. For 𝐿 source bytes and 𝑁
carrier symbols, 𝐺 = 8𝐿/𝑁 for authenticated, exact, conforming recovery, and zero
otherwise. Text uses bits/token and PNG bits/channel; the latter multiplied by three
gives bits/pixel. Padding, framing and completion consume capacity without increas-
ing useful bits. Recovery intervals are two-sided 95% Clopper–Pearson bounds under
a Bernoulli working model. Continuous quantities and AUCs use the retained 2,000
stratified group-bootstrap draws, keeping paired methods and owned controls together
within image class or text-length band. Percentile intervals describe this fixed alloca-
tion. Boundary paired-binary comparisons also retain conservative marginal bounds;
collapsed bootstrap intervals are not certainty.

tance requires exact eligible identifiers, rank order, float64 probability streams, PNG
bytes and recovered bytes. The separate photograph study uses six development and
twenty test BSDS500 covers, selected by lowest numeric identifier, converted to RGB
and center-cropped without resizing [1]. Arms share texts and positions; comparisons
are descriptive paired summaries.


5     Results

5.1   Recovery, Useful Rate and Finite Capacity

The 120 outcomes comprise 100 exact, authenticated, conforming recoveries and 20
arithmetic-text failures (Fig. 2). Fixed and gated coding recover 20/20 in both directions;
A1 recovers 20/20 PNG and 0/20 text payloads. Corresponding 95% recovery bounds
are [83.16%, 100%] and [0%, 16.84%].
    Fixed text uses 584 packet tokens plus 32 completion tokens. Gating adds a mean
39.8 skipped positions: relative to fixed coding, mean goodput falls by 0.2000 bits/token
[0.1805, 0.2192] and charged latency rises by 23.96 s [20.34, 27.58]. PNG goodput is
identical across coders, 0.25054 bits/channel [0.24731, 0.25349], or 0.75161 bits/pixel,
because all methods recover the same sources and fill the same canvas. Equal achieved
rates do not imply equal capacity. Fixed PNG uses 584 packet channels; gated PNG
adds 100.5 skips on average; A1 stops after a mean 597.85 channels, including 89.65
zero-bit steps. Text payloads leave a mean 162.8 padding bytes. Original charged pair
times for fixed/gated/A1 are 123.33/147.29/826.57 s for text and 178.18/178.53/179.01
s for PNG, including failures.
    All A1 text cases reach the 2,048-token cap with only 581–2,216 recovered packet-
prefix bits (mean 1,456.85), below the 2,336-bit target. None authenticates; partial bits
are not useful delivery. Independent interval and prefix audits agree across all forty
prospective arithmetic cases. Two failed texts contain one and three isolated unchanged

5.2   Detection Depends on Carrier and Observer

Both fixed and gated text have AUC 1.000 for both scores, with bootstrap interval
[1.000, 1.000]: every steganographic score lies above the ordinary range. This complete
empirical separation materially limits concealment claims, despite exact recovery. It
does not imply perfect detection in a broader population. Failed A1 text has primary
AUC 0.380 [0.260, 0.503]; its below-chance orientation is retained, and it delivers no
payload.
     Correct-context PNG mean-surprisal AUC is 0.8850 [0.7425, 0.9850] for fixed cod-
ing, 0.8300 [0.7000, 0.9375] for gating and 0.5575 [0.3750, 0.7426] for A1. Secondary
log-rank AUCs are 0.8400, 0.7700 and 0.5750, respectively. Each comparison contains
20 steganographic artifacts and 20 unique controls, with none excluded. Arithmetic PNG
thus combines exact recovery with lower observed separation for these scores, but its
wide intervals do not establish security against arbitrary observers.
     Under the mismatched row, all 80 PNGs remain scorable (Fig. 3). Fixed AUC falls to
0.6975, a paired change of −0.1875 [−0.2775, −0.1000]. Gated AUC becomes 0.7475,
a change of −0.0825 [−0.1625, 0.0100], and A1 becomes 0.5925, a change of 0.0350
[−0.0225, 0.1050]. Only the fixed reduction is resolved by its paired interval. Primary
fixed and gated mismatch intervals still exclude 0.5; the fixed secondary interval includes
it. These intervals do not establish a between-method sensitivity difference. Moreover,
the correct row comes from generated imagery and the alternate row from random RGB
values: context correctness is not isolated from row statistics.

5.3   Execution Cost and the Photograph Baseline

CUDA-graph replay yields a 5.133-fold reduction in matched fixed-rank latency across
nine timing pairs on three development payloads (Table 1a). All eleven comparisons
preserve exact probability/rank streams and artifacts, and all 22 carriers recover correctly.
Graph setup is included in charged time. Reserved memory rises from 516 to 744 MiB.
This stack-specific development result does not replace the original prospective timings.
    Both photograph arms recover 6/6 development and 20/20 held-out messages (Ta-
ble 1b). PSNR uses RGB mean-square error and peak 255; SSIM averages channels
with an 11 × 11 Gaussian (𝜎 = 1.5), valid centers, population covariance and 𝐾1 = .01,
𝐾2 = .03. The model arm changes at most two channel levels in the observed sample;
parity changes at most one. Its paired mean PSNR is 0.144 dB lower than parity’s,
with substantially greater runtime. All 46,720 selected learned partitions differ from
parity even allowing label swaps, yet distinct partitions confer no demonstrated recov-
ery, fidelity or cost advantage. Fixed-context tile evaluation differs from autoregressive
                                                                                           9


generation, precluding a matched speedup claim. Photograph fidelity establishes neither
concealment nor editing resistance; generated-PNG detector results do not transfer here.


6   Discussion and Limitations
Saved-file recovery requires reproducible serialization. A separate development com-
parison recovers 40/40 cases with complete-prefix filtering versus 22/40 with singleton
filtering, using twenty payloads under two prompts. The eighteen failures remain un-
repaired; forty cases are not forty independent payloads. Lossless PNG resaves were
checked, but JPEG conversion, cropping and paraphrasing were not evaluated.
     Gating trades capacity and time for lower average surprisal while leaving the tested
text scores completely separated. A1’s lower PNG separation applies only to the stated
observer and sample; incomplete text information cannot count as delivery. Faster image
inference cannot supply missing bits or remove text’s CPU filtering bottleneck. Photo-
graph fidelity and distinct learned partitions likewise do not establish concealment.
     Two pinned models, twenty groups per direction and one alternate context limit
generalization. Shared controls and timing repetitions remain dependent. Decoding
requires compatible numerical execution, models and keys. Full profiles and evidence
are retained; the main claims require no supplementary PDF.


7   Conclusion
Generative models support exact cross-medium recovery under a shared protocol, but
usefulness depends separately on capacity, reproducible decoding, detectability and
cost. Serialization consistency is essential for saved text; insufficient capacity prevents
delivery even when endpoints agree. Exact recovery can coexist with easy detection,
whose measurement depends on observer assumptions. Acceleration is useful when
decoding remains consistent. Learned image constructions also need simple alternatives:
here parity recovers the same messages with slightly better mean fidelity and much lower
cost.
