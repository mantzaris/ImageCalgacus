 The soft afternoon breeze riles no leaf on a single tree: I step from gravel onto forest trail; a soft carpet unfurls at my feet.
Soft. Everything soft here, in this moment at least: the breeze is not fierce,
just present , rustling only my hat
not ridding branches from leaves;
no, even their tangle is serene;
their gentle touch is not urgent
no leaves quake, but softly sway; even roots, ancient as
rockface
seeps deep within dark humus—silvery shadows;
where sun’s filtered whispers fade; the trail curves and unwinds: my thoughts follow this gentle pace:
where ancient trunks lean
as worn columns: the silence,
so soft—like a breath
barely whispers; no sudden shock disturbs.
In contrast a single leaf,
like translucent membrane—delicacy: my
sneaken—stretches: my pace
me; and this still-summer-air,
in silence. This silence. In. (It seems a world.) My footfall mashes only the first leaf; the next ones bend, without snapping. This
gentle touch:
as though I step only among clouds—and they softly shift; beneath me now:
where forest, in the distance—blossoming green;
the silence, in the forest—fuses, and
all the stillness—unspoken.
It becomes as a thought
which is, before it is expressed—yet still,
a thing which has not
taken its true,
final breath: this forest: an
unfinished song.
